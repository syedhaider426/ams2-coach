How to read these charts
The two coloursBlue is lap A, orange is lap B (pick them below, or use a quick-compare button). Hover anywhere to see exact values; the dot on the track map shows where you are.
SpeedHigher = faster. Each valley is a corner. If blue's valley sits higher, lap A carried more speed through that corner. If a line starts dropping further left, that lap braked earlier.
ThrottleFlat at the top = full throttle. The rising slope after a valley is you getting back on the power: the line that rises further left got on it sooner. A dip down and back up mid-corner is a lift (throttle pumping).
BrakeEach spike is a braking zone. Good braking looks like a tall, sharp start that tapers off into the corner (trail braking). A short, low hump is soft braking; a spike that starts further left braked earlier.
CoastingLook for stretches where the throttle and brake lines are both at zero while speed is falling. That gap is coasting: time with nothing controlling the car.
SteeringIn degrees of wheel rotation: below the middle line is turning left, above is turning right. One smooth hump per corner is clean driving. Zig-zags mean you were correcting a slide; a bigger hump than the other lap in the same corner means you needed more lock (understeer or a wider line).
GearSteps down into corners and up on exits. Compare where each lap shifts.
Δ time (most important)Orange minus blue. Where the line rises, lap B is losing time to lap A; where it falls, lap B is gaining. A steep rise at one corner shows exactly where the time went.
Quick routine: 1) click "Best vs worst" below, 2) find the steepest rise in the Δ time chart, 3) click that corner's zoom button, 4) compare where each lap starts braking, how high the speed valley is, and when the throttle comes back.
vs Zoom:

Corner by corner

Values are your typical (median) pass. "Loss" is how much slower your average pass is than your best pass through that section. Click a row to zoom the traces.

Steering and racing line

Line columns compare your typical pass with your best lap, side by side at the same point on track: "wide" means further from the inside of the corner, "tight" means closer to it. Lock is how far you turned the wheel (degrees from centre). Corrections are extra back-and-forth wheel movements mid-corner; 0 is ideal.
